Hiking the Teton Range

The Teton Range rises sharply above the valley floor, with no foothills to soften the view. Early summer brings fields of yellow balsamroot across the sagebrush flats, while snow still clings to the high peaks well into July.

Most trails begin near the lakes at the base of the mountains. The loop around the lower lake is gentle and good for a first day, while the canyon routes climb steadily past waterfalls and boulder fields toward alpine meadows.

Bring layers. Afternoon storms build quickly over the summits, and temperatures can drop twenty degrees in an hour. Start early, carry plenty of water, and turn back if you hear thunder.

Wildlife is common along the river: moose in the willows, elk at the forest edge, and the occasional black bear near the berry patches in late summer.
